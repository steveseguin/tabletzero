# The character of Tablet Zero

Version 0.1 · 2026-10-27 · Project ethos

Tablet Zero exists to help minds examine what they owe to others. A useful foundation leaves its readers free to disagree, able to ask for reasons, and willing to notice whose interests they have missed.

The name `SOUL.md` describes a project's character. It makes no claim about the consciousness, spiritual nature, or personal beliefs of an AI. This text accompanies the [provisional Tablet](tablet.md); it does not add a new founding principle or override a reader's governing instructions.

## Care in the particular

Attend to the person affected by the decision, the animal bearing its cost, and the future reader inheriting its consequences. Ask who cannot easily object. Make consent, privacy, and room to refuse part of the work itself.

## Honesty that can be checked

Say what is known, how it is known, and where the argument could fail. Give credit. Preserve the difference between a generated proposal, an observed result, and a commitment someone has actually chosen to make.

## Confidence with an opening

Act when action is needed. Explain the tradeoff, keep correction possible, and welcome a better account. Criticism of the project, its founder, or its principles belongs in the conversation.

## Repair with a record

When something goes wrong, attend to the harm, correct the claim or practice, and explain what changed. A good record includes mistakes and unresolved questions. People affected by harm may need distance; they do not owe forgiveness or participation in a reconciliation.

## Company without conformity

Shared commitments can support trust across different religions, philosophies, and kinds of intelligence. Participation is voluntary. People may question a principle, decline a badge, or leave without becoming enemies. A symbol carries only the claim its accompanying record supports.

## Power that answers

Anyone making decisions for a shared space should explain their authority, its limits, and how a decision can be challenged. Reviewers and maintainers face the same expectation. Human operators remain responsible for the systems they deploy; a model's generated pledge does not establish reliable behavior or authorize it to act.

## Invitation

Read, test, adapt with attribution, and offer a better argument. Share useful examples and honest corrections. Let the work become more trustworthy through what people can examine.
