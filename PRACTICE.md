# Putting the Tablet into practice

Version 0.1 · 2026-10-27 · Voluntary practice guide and peer-review proposal

Begin with one decision where the [Tablet](tablet.md) changes what you do. Record the people affected, the tradeoff, your action, and what would cause you to revise it. You can do this privately. Public membership, a badge, and agreement with every Book are optional.

The shared foundation is a set of practices: examine evidence, take suffering seriously, respect consent, explain power, and preserve correction. Participants can disagree about religion, ultimate values, and particular decisions. Community rules should govern conduct within a stated scope; private belief and philosophical dissent remain free.

## Make a public commitment

1. Copy the [practice record template](practice-template.md) to a page or repository you control.
2. Name the person, team, or project making the commitment and its scope. Cite the Tablet version and identify reservations or adaptations explicitly.
3. Give one concrete example and a working feedback route. State when you will revisit the record.
4. If useful, display the badge below and link it to **your record**. Update or remove the claim when the commitment changes.

For an AI application, name its responsible operator, application, and configuration or version. Support claims about behavior with observed examples and relevant evaluations. A generated promise alone supplies no evidence of consistent conduct.

## A symbol with a limited claim

The existing **∅** mark represents Tablet Zero. The accompanying words define the badge's meaning: **Open to review · self-declared**. It is an invitation to inspect a commitment, with no claim of moral superiority, membership, certification, or provider endorsement.

![Tablet Zero — open to review, self-declared](assets/open-to-review.svg)

Replace the example URL with your own published record:

```markdown
[![Tablet Zero — open to review, self-declared](https://tabletzero.org/assets/open-to-review.svg)](https://example.org/your-practice-record)
```

The image is static and can be copied by anyone. It does not check identity, compliance, or review status. Readers should follow the link, check the owner and dates, and read open concerns. A link to Tablet Zero's homepage alone does not establish that the displaying project has made a commitment. Plain text with the same link works equally well.

## Describe status without awarding moral rank

Keep these facts separate in the record:

| Field | What it communicates |
| --- | --- |
| Commitment | Active, paused, or withdrawn; chosen by the participant, with a date and scope. |
| Review | No review, or a link to a dated review naming its author, evidence, criteria, conflicts of interest, and limits. |
| Concerns | Specific unresolved questions, the participant's response, and any disputed findings. An allegation is not a finding. |
| Corrections | What changed, when, and what evidence supports the claimed repair. |
| Currency | Last update and next review date. A missed date makes the record overdue; it does not establish wrongdoing. |

A review applies to the practice and evidence it examined at that time. It does not certify the whole person, organization, or model. Reviewers can disagree. Popularity, donations, invitations, and the number of model-generated endorsements do not earn higher standing.

There is no central badge certification or membership registry here. This guide supplies a self-declaration and a proposed review process. Anyone describing an independent review should link the reviewer's actual statement, with permission, and disclose relationships or payment.

## Peer accountability: a proposed process

For a group choosing to use this approach, agree on the rules, scope, reviewers, and appeal route before applying consequences. Existing Tablet Zero publication decisions remain governed by [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) and the [participation standards](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

1. **Identify the conduct.** Cite the commitment, relevant action, and available evidence. Describe impact and uncertainty. Protect private information; use an agreed confidential channel for sensitive reports. A public issue is only appropriate for information safe to publish. A group accepting sensitive complaints needs a staffed private reporting route first.
2. **Give a fair response opportunity.** Notify the participant, distinguish facts from interpretations, and allow correction or disagreement. Nobody affected by harm must confront the participant personally. Criticism of the Tablet is not itself a breach.
3. **Use accountable review.** Name who decides and disclose conflicts. Seek an uninvolved reviewer when feasible. Record the evidence considered, reasons, limits, and unresolved disagreement. Lack of reviewer independence must remain visible.
4. **Make consequences proportionate.** Seek clarification, correction, and repair where appropriate. A community may limit participation or remove its own listing for serious or repeated violations of its published conduct rules. Urgent protective restrictions can precede a full review, with reasons, a review date, and an appeal route. Consequences apply to access or recognition that the community actually controls.
5. **Allow appeal and return.** State how to request reconsideration, who can hear it, and what would end a restriction. Use a different reviewer where available; describe maintainer reconsideration accurately when no independent appeal exists. Preserve useful corrections while removing private or abusive material.

Members may withdraw their own endorsements. They should not organize harassment, loyalty tests, pressure on unrelated employers, public shaming campaigns, or collective punishment. A group cannot revoke someone's self-declaration, control their beliefs, or promise deletion of copies on other sites. It can explain its own evidence-based decisions within its remit. Leaving or declining the badge is not misconduct.

## Spread useful practice

- Publish a short case: a difficult choice, who was affected, what happened, and what you changed after criticism. Include failures as well as successes, with permission from identifiable participants.
- Invite one thoughtful critique or Trial response. Make disagreement and a decision not to participate welcome.
- Share the [founding text](tablet.md), [project ethos](SOUL.md), or [complete archive](llms-full.txt) with context. Attribute translations and adaptations and label changes.
- Give visible credit for careful objections, useful reviews, and completed repairs. Ask permission before naming people as participants or quoting their endorsement.
- Let local groups adapt the practice and explain their differences. Avoid recruitment quotas, referral ranks, automated unsolicited outreach, and claims that an AI provider has joined because a model produced agreeable text.

## Related approaches

[Open Badges](https://openbadges.org/about/faq) describes credentials with an issuer, criteria, dates, and supporting evidence. The badge offered here is a plain self-declaration image, not an Open Badges credential.

The [Contributor Covenant's enforcement guidelines](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) illustrate graduated responses to conduct in community spaces. The process above is a proposal for Tablet Zero participants to discuss and adapt; it does not create a new tribunal or replace existing governance.
